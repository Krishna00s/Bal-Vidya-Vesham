# Bal Vidyavasham × Manjurmati — Project Master Brief

> **Purpose:** The single source of truth for restarting the public-facing school website from scratch. Read this file before planning or changing code. Do not recreate the old implementation or revive a scattered documentation system. Keep the project visual, factual, maintainable, and built in deliberate phases.

---

## 1. Project Goal

Create a distinctive, polished, photo-led website for **Bal Vidyavasham** and **Manjurmati**. The two schools should feel connected through one coherent digital experience while retaining their separate identities and age groups.

The result should feel art-directed by a thoughtful designer—not like a generic school template, an AI-generated landing page, or a collection of repeated cards. Visual storytelling should do most of the work. Keep copy purposeful and concise; let authentic school photography, composition, typography, whitespace, and carefully choreographed motion carry the experience.

The homepage should tell most of the schools’ story without forcing visitors through walls of text. It must be practical for families, easy to use on phones, accessible, and fast.

**Do not start by coding the whole website.** Work one phase at a time, inspect the result, and wait for approval before proceeding.

Use only these facts as confirmed:

## 3. Creative Direction

### Overall feeling
- Art-directed, warm, human, editorial, tactile, expressive, and contemporary.
- A coherent visual journey, not a stack of unrelated sections.
- Photography-led, with fewer but more memorable compositions.
- Intentional asymmetry balanced by a clear underlying layout system.
- Spacious, but not wasteful or randomly oversized.
- Playful details used with restraint; suitable for a real school and its families.

### Current palette direction
Move away from the previous dark-green-led visual direction. Use a warm, paper-inspired palette as the starting point:
- **Warm ivory / parchment:** primary canvas and light sections.
- **Burnt terracotta / clay / muted rust:** selective emphasis, buttons, small accents, and occasional graphic details.
- **Ink / charcoal:** primary text and strong contrast.
- **Soft stone / warm taupe:** supporting surfaces and dividers.
- **Natural colours in authentic school photography:** allowed to provide additional colour.

These are directional descriptions, not permission to introduce a different random accent in every section. Define a restrained token palette and use it consistently. Do not make dark green the dominant brand colour. Do not force every section to use every colour.

### Typography
- Use an expressive, highly legible editorial serif for selected display headings.
- Pair it with a clean, readable sans-serif for body text, navigation, labels, and utility UI.
- Establish a clear type scale and hierarchy.
- Avoid oversized typography everywhere. Large type should have a compositional reason and remain usable on small screens.

### Visual motifs and texture
Potential tools include paper-like surfaces, subtle torn or irregular edges, fine hand-drawn annotations, restrained ink/botanical illustrations, and small editorial details. Use them only when they strengthen the story and are executed consistently.

Avoid:
- Excessive scribbles, leaves, doodles, or fake handmade decoration.
- Heavy grunge filters and noisy paper textures.
- Random blobs, gradients, glows, and decorative shapes without a clear purpose.
- Overlapping elements that obscure content or look accidental.
- The same three-card grid repeated in every section.
- A generic hero with text on one side and a stock image on the other unless the composition genuinely warrants it.
- Generic “premium” styling that could belong to any school, agency, or SaaS template.

## 4. Composition and Storytelling

Think like an editorial art director. Plan the page as one continuous visual composition, with each section responding to the section before it.

Explore:
- Images that cross section boundaries intentionally.
- Varied image sizes and crops rather than identical cards.
- Alternating dense and quiet moments.
- A few strong full-width photographic moments.
- Text that sits naturally within the visual composition instead of always being confined to a card.
- Deliberate alignment, margins, rhythm, and whitespace.
- Section transitions that feel connected, not like abrupt stacked rectangles.
- Different visual treatments for the junior and senior schools while preserving one shared design language.

The fact that the schools are opposite one another may appear in a small contextual story, map/visit detail, or visual connection if useful. **Do not build the whole concept around “Two Schools, One Road,” “Two Schools. Different Beginnings. A Shared Tomorrow,” or any other fixed slogan.** Headlines and the overall narrative must emerge from verified content and approved art direction, not from treating the road as the central idea.

### Suggested homepage content territories
These are content territories, not a mandatory section order or rigid wireframe. Design the best sequence after auditing available media.

1. **Opening / hero:** establish the schools’ identity and atmosphere with authentic, compelling school imagery, clear navigation, concise copy, and a useful next action.
2. **Shared introduction:** explain the relationship between the two schools without implying automatic progression or guaranteed admission.
3. **Bal Vidyavasham:** make the junior-school identity clear, with Nursery–V and English-medium information.
4. **Manjurmati:** make the senior-school identity clear, with Classes VI–X, both medium streams, and JAC affiliation.
5. **Life at school:** a photo-led editorial sequence featuring genuine school activities, events, sports, celebrations, trips, student work, and other available moments.
6. **Community / values / approach:** include only statements the school can verify and stand behind; avoid generic claims.
7. **Practical information and contact:** use verified contact and visit information only.
8. **Closing moment / footer:** finish the visual story cleanly, with useful navigation and verified details.

Do not force every territory into a separate box. Combine, reorder, or omit sections where the available content and visual story call for it. Do not fabricate content to fill a planned section.

## 5. Authentic Media and Asset Rules

Authentic school media is a major part of the design, not an afterthought.

Before choosing the layout:
1. Inspect the repository and existing asset folders.
2. Inventory images, videos, logos, portraits, event photos, and other media.
3. Identify which school and event each asset appears to represent, but do not assume identity or context when unclear.
4. Check image quality, dimensions, duplicates, orientation, and suitability for the intended crop.
5. Create a simple asset inventory/report identifying strong candidates, uncertain assets, and missing media.

Use genuine school assets wherever possible. Do not use AI-generated students, invented buildings, fabricated uniforms, fake signage, or generic stock school photos as though they depict these actual schools. Concept images and mockups are for art direction only, not factual evidence. If an asset’s authenticity or context is uncertain, flag it for confirmation.

Use image crops thoughtfully and responsively. Preserve subjects’ faces and important content. Avoid loading large original images unnecessarily; use appropriate formats, dimensions, lazy loading, and responsive sources where practical.

Do not delete or rename existing assets during the audit. Do not create a large new image library without approval.

## 6. School Life and Future Growth

The website should be ready in its content structure to showcase:
- School events and celebrations.
- Sports and extracurricular activities.
- Trips, outings, and hands-on experiences.
- Student artwork, projects, and work.
- Verified achievements and results.
- Participation and community moments.
- Short videos, reels, and social-media links where available.

Do not invent awards or results. When achievements cannot be verified, highlight the genuine activity or participation rather than turning it into an achievement claim.

A future School Life / Stories area may support filters such as school, category, and year. Keep the structure extendable, but do not build a CMS or complex filtering system prematurely. The public-facing experience comes first.

## 7. Technical Baseline

First inspect the repository and actual environment. Do not assume this document’s expected stack matches every installed version.

The project has previously used React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS, GSAP, Lenis, and Lucide icons. Verify the current `package.json`, lockfile, scripts, framework versions, Tailwind setup, and installed dependencies before changing anything. Reuse the existing setup where sound. Do not upgrade, downgrade, replace, or add dependencies without a clear reason and explicit reporting.

Technical priorities:
- Responsive from small mobile screens upward.
- Strong semantic HTML and accessibility.
- Keyboard-accessible navigation and controls.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Fast initial rendering and optimized media.
- No unnecessary client-side JavaScript.
- No layout shifts from images or fonts.
- Motion that degrades gracefully on mobile and lower-powered devices.
- Clean component boundaries and reusable design tokens.
- Remove dead code only after confirming it is genuinely unused.
- Avoid premature abstraction and needless dependencies.
- No console errors, broken links, or obvious responsive defects.

Use GSAP only where motion adds meaning. Use Lenis only if it genuinely improves the experience and works well with accessibility, touch, and reduced-motion preferences. Do not animate every element simply because an animation library is available.

### Motion principles
Motion should support the visual story:
- A considered opening sequence rather than an overloaded animation spectacle.
- Gentle, purposeful reveals and transitions.
- Image movement or masking only where it improves the composition.
- Clear timing, easing, and hierarchy.
- No scroll-jacking, forced delays, or animations that block visitors from accessing content.
- Avoid excessive parallax, constant floating elements, and motion that harms readability.
- Ensure all content remains available when motion is reduced or JavaScript effects fail.
- Clean up GSAP timelines, ScrollTriggers, Lenis instances, event listeners, and observers appropriately.

## 8. Scope Boundaries

### Build now
- Public-facing website structure.
- Homepage visual direction and responsive implementation.
- Shared identity plus distinct junior/senior school identities.
- Authentic media presentation.
- Verified school information.
- Accessible navigation and useful contact/visit pathways once details are verified.
- A scalable structure for future school stories.

### Do not build yet
- CMS or admin dashboard.
- Authentication and role-based access.
- Supabase/database integration.
- Admissions portal, student/parent accounts, fee payments, or backend workflows.
- Booking systems or forms that imply operational capability without an approved backend.
- Unverified maps, live locations, contact details, or institutional claims.
- A large set of inner pages before the homepage design language is approved.

Keep future expansion in mind, but do not let speculative features compromise the quality or speed of the public website.

## 9. Required Working Method

Work in small, reviewable phases. Do not jump ahead.

### Phase 0 — Repository and media audit
- Read this master brief completely.
- Inspect the current Git branch, working tree, repository structure, existing documentation, `package.json`, scripts, and installed dependencies.
- Inventory public assets and any school media folders.
- Identify the current entry point, global styles, layout, and any existing components.
- Run existing build/lint checks if available and record the baseline.
- Report what is reusable, what is obsolete, what facts or assets are missing, and any risks.
- **Do not edit or delete files in this phase.**

### Phase 1 — Art direction and visual plan
Based on the audit, propose:
- A restrained colour and typography token system.
- A clear visual concept and design rationale.
- A homepage sequence and compositional plan.
- Which authentic images belong in which moments, including why each image fits.
- The intended transition between major sections.
- Mobile-specific composition changes.
- A motion plan with reduced-motion alternatives.
- Any missing media or factual details requiring confirmation.

Use the latest user-approved warm paper / ivory, terracotta, charcoal, and natural-photography direction as the starting point. Do not simply reproduce a generated concept image pixel-for-pixel; translate its useful art direction into a real, responsive site using authentic assets. If a reference image or video is not available inside the project or current coding-agent context, say so—never pretend to have inspected it.

Stop and wait for approval before implementation.

### Phase 2 — Foundation and first viewport
After approval, implement only the approved design foundation and opening/hero experience:
- Design tokens, fonts, base styles, header/navigation, hero composition, responsive behaviour, and only the opening transition that is needed.
- Use real assets selected during the audit.
- Verify at mobile, tablet, and desktop sizes.
- Build and lint; report the exact files changed and any remaining issues.
- Stop and wait for review.

### Phase 3 — Homepage story
Only after approval of Phase 2, implement the next agreed group of sections. Preserve visual rhythm and vary compositions intentionally. Test the full scroll sequence and responsive layouts.

### Phase 4 — Polish and verification
After the main structure is approved:
- Refine motion and transitions.
- Verify accessibility, reduced motion, keyboard interactions, responsive layouts, performance, image loading, links, and factual accuracy.
- Run build/lint and fix issues caused by the implementation.
- Provide a concise change report and any remaining verification questions.

Further pages or backend features should be planned separately after the public website is reviewed.

## 10. Git and Change Safety

- Start by reporting the current branch and working-tree status.
- Do not reset, force-push, rebase, delete branches, or overwrite user work.
- Do not commit, push, merge, or deploy unless explicitly asked.
- Preserve unrelated work and existing user assets.
- Before any cleanup, show what will be removed and why; get approval for destructive changes.
- Keep changes scoped to the active phase.
- Report commands that failed and do not claim tests passed unless they were actually run.

## 11. Definition of Done for Each Phase

A phase is complete only when:
- Its scope is implemented or audited as requested.
- The actual result has been checked at relevant viewport sizes when implementation is involved.
- Build/lint checks have been run where available and results are reported accurately.
- Factual claims are traceable to verified information.
- Existing assets and unrelated work are preserved.
- The change report lists files changed, tests performed, known issues, and the next proposed phase.
- The coding agent stops and waits for approval rather than automatically continuing.

## 12. Non-Negotiable Principles

1. **Design with intention.** Every major image, line of text, overlap, transition, and empty space needs a reason.
2. **Visuals before volume.** Prefer a few memorable, authentic moments over long generic copy.
3. **One coherent world, two distinct schools.** Shared design language; clear differences in school stage and identity.
4. **No invented facts.** Missing information stays unresolved until confirmed.
5. **No generic template repetition.** Do not solve every section with the same grid, card, and heading arrangement.
6. **No random colours.** Maintain the restrained warm palette and use accents deliberately.
7. **No dominant dark-green direction.** The current starting direction is warm ivory/paper, terracotta, charcoal, and authentic photography.
8. **The road is not the theme.** The schools’ physical proximity is a supporting fact only.
9. **No AI imagery pretending to be real school media.**
10. **One phase at a time.** Audit, propose, obtain approval, implement, test, and review.
